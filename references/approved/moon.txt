The Dark Side of the Moon

We only ever see one side of the moon, but why?
It's not because the moon doesn't spin. It does.
It turns exactly once for every trip around Earth, so it always keeps the same face toward us.
The other side is often called the dark side, but it isn't really dark.
It gets just as much sunlight as ours. We just never get to see it.
Long ago, the moon spun faster, but Earth's gravity pulled on it, and that pull slowly braked its spin until it matched its orbit.
Astronomers call it tidal locking.
And most big moons in the solar system are locked to their planets the same way.
